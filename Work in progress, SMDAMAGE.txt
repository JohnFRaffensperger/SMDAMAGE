Audit: Why run_SMDAMAGE_short_auctions (YEARS_IN_AUCTION=200) differs from run_SMDAMAGE
Date: 2026-08-01
============================================================================================

SETUP
-----
run_SMDAMAGE calls Solve_SMDAMAGE with tau=1.7, fitted Wpt (calibration_scenario_id=1).
run_SMDAMAGE_short_auctions is set to YEARS_IN_AUCTION=200, local_tau=1.7, same scenario.
With YEARS_IN_AUCTION=200 the intent is "one big 200-year auction ≈ the long-run model."
Key parameters from defaults_and_utilities.py:
  getStartYear()            = 2025
  getFirstConstrainedYear() = 2125  (BeginConstraintYear)
  getNumber_of_bid_years()  = 250   → getBidPeriods() = [2025.0 … 2274.0]
  getPulseDataLength()      = 296   → getModelPeriods() = [2025.0 … 2320.0]

Confirmed from smdamage_solutions.db:
  Scenario 11 (run_SMDAMAGE, tau=1.7, fitted Wpt): objective/1000 = $179,713 B  ($179.7 T)
  Scenario 18 (short auctions, tau=1.7, fitted Wpt, HiGHS solver): obj/1000 = $160,855 B
  Scenario 27 (short auctions, most recent correct-looking run): obj/1000 = $178,854 B
  Scenarios 21, 25, 26 (short auctions with HiGHS): net_revenue ≈ -$738 T to -$87 quad
    → astronomical garbage values, clear solver mis-parse.

============================================================================================
FINDING 1 (PRIMARY): WRONG SOLVER — PULP_CBC_CMD('HiGHS', msg=0)
============================================================================================

Line 634 of SMDAMAGE revenue neutral.py:
  solve_status = LpStatus[SMDAMAGE.solve(PULP_CBC_CMD('HiGHS', msg=0))]

The first positional argument to PULP_CBC_CMD is `path` — the filesystem path to the CBC
binary. Passing the string 'HiGHS' causes PuLP to attempt to run an executable called
'HiGHS' with CBC-style command-line arguments. If HiGHS is installed on the system PATH,
it may actually run but produce output in a format that PuLP's CBC output parser cannot
read correctly. PuLP would then populate variable values with zeroes or prior-iteration
garbage, and the computed net_revenue (which multiplies variable values by dual prices)
would be nonsensical.

Evidence from DB:
  - Scenarios 21, 25, 26 show net_revenue of –$738 trillion to –$87 quadrillion.
  - Their objective values (50,071 B and 153–167 thousand B) are inconsistent with each
    other and with correctly solved runs (all clustering near 160–180 thousand B).
  - The user's reported "$25.41 trillion" is almost certainly net_revenue or a misread
    objective from one of these broken solves.

Correct invocation (as in Solve_SMDAMAGE):
  solve_status = LpStatus[SMDAMAGE.solve(PULP_CBC_CMD(msg=0, options=['-threads', '8']))]

This is the #1 cause. Fix this first; the structural differences below are secondary.

============================================================================================
FINDING 2: MISSING 50 BID YEARS (2225–2274)
============================================================================================

With YEARS_IN_AUCTION=200 and loop `for startyear in range(2025, 2026)`:
  BidPeriods = [2025.0 … 2224.0]  (200 years)
  AllBidPeriods = getBidPeriods() = [2025.0 … 2274.0]  (250 years)
  Missing: 2225–2274  (50 years, 20% of all bid years)

vpt and qapt in short_auctions only cover years 2025–2224. Years 2225–2274 have no
decision variables at all. In Solve_SMDAMAGE, vpt covers all 250 bid years.

Impact on objective:
  Emitter payments and removal costs for 50 years are absent. Since the auction is net-
  revenue-positive (emitters pay more than removers cost), omitting 50 years reduces the
  total. Direction: short_auctions objective LOWER.

Impact on temperature constraints:
  For constraint periods t ≥ 2225, no removal variables exist to compensate for post-2224
  warming. Whatever warming remains after year 2224 (from long-lived GHGs) cannot be
  offset. The model must compensate by mandating more aggressive removal in 2025–2224
  to meet constraints at t = 2225–2320. Direction: constraint is TIGHTER → more costly
  removal → objective LOWER.

Comparison of the Temp_t constraint by period:
  t ≤ 2224:  both models sum over the same u ≤ t (within their respective vpt coverage).
             Identical.
  2224 < t ≤ 2274:  Solve_SMDAMAGE can use u up to t (e.g., u=2250 for t=2250).
             Short_auctions cannot use u > 2224.
  t > 2274:  Both are limited to u ≤ 2274 by AllBidPeriods; identical at that point
             (short_auctions was further limited to u ≤ 2224).

Fix: extend the loop to cover all bid years, e.g.:
  for startyear in range(int(getStartYear()), int(max(AllBidPeriods)) + 1, YEARS_IN_AUCTION):
With YEARS_IN_AUCTION=200 that gives [2025, 2225]; the second auction would pick up
the remaining 50 years in BidPeriods and cover them.

============================================================================================
FINDING 3: TAU APPLIED UNIFORMLY ACROSS ALL BID YEARS VS SPLIT AT BeginConstraintYear
============================================================================================

Solve_SMDAMAGE builds taxed_exprs with a split at u = BeginConstraintYear = 2125:

  taxed_temp[t] = tau * sum(W[p,t-u]*v[p,u]  for emitters with u ≤ 2124)   ← τ=1.7
               + 1.0 * sum(W[p,t-u]*v[p,u]  for emitters with u ≥ 2125)   ← τ=1.0
               + 1.0 * sum(W[p,t-u]*v[p,u]  for removers)

run_SMDAMAGE_short_auctions applies local_tau uniformly to ALL emitters in BidPeriods:

  taxed_temp[t] = local_tau * sum(W[p,t-u]*v[p,u]  for emitters in 2025–2224)  ← τ=1.7
               + 1.0      * sum(W[p,t-u]*v[p,u]  for removers in 2025–2224)

Difference: For emitters in years 2125–2224 (100 years):
  Solve_SMDAMAGE:  τ = 1.0  (no surcharge; auction has cleared the target year)
  Short_auctions:  τ = 1.7  (surcharge still applied post-2124)

With τ=1.7 on post-2124 emissions, the constraint at t ∈ ConstraintPeriods requires
1.7× cooling offset instead of 1.0× for those years. This is strictly more restrictive.
More removal must be purchased → objective LOWER for short_auctions.

This is in the OPPOSITE direction from the observed result (short_auctions shows higher
value). Therefore, finding 2 + finding 3 alone would produce short_auctions ≤ run_SMDAMAGE.
The ONLY way short_auctions can show a wildly higher objective is Finding 1 (wrong solver).

Quantitative rough estimate:
  Scenario 27 (short_auctions, appears correct): obj = $178,854 B
  Scenario 11 (run_SMDAMAGE):                   obj = $179,713 B
  Difference: –$859 B (short_auctions is 0.5% lower than run_SMDAMAGE)
  This small gap is consistent with the 50 missing years + stricter tau offsetting each other.
  Note: scenario 27 likely used a different YEARS_IN_AUCTION or loop range that happened to
  include more bid years; without knowing its exact parameters the comparison is approximate.

============================================================================================
FINDING 4: FIXED PERIODS TAX USES scenario.tau NOT local_tau (MINOR FOR SINGLE-AUCTION CASE)
============================================================================================

In the taxed temperature constraint for auction k, fixed-period terms use scenario.tau:

  + sum(scenario.tau * W * Fixed_Vpt  for emitters in FixedPeriods with u ≤ BeginConstraintYear)
  + sum(1.0         * W * Fixed_Vpt  for emitters in FixedPeriods with u > BeginConstraintYear)
  + sum(1.0         * W * Fixed_Vpt  for removers in FixedPeriods)

For the first auction (FixedPeriods=[], single-auction case with YEARS_IN_AUCTION=200),
these terms are all zero and there is no impact. But for multi-auction runs with 51-year
windows, if local_tau is adjusted dynamically between auctions, prior-period emissions are
re-weighted using scenario.tau (a constant), while current-period emissions use local_tau.
This is a deliberate design choice to make future auctions account for accumulated warming
from past decisions. No bug here, but worth documenting.

============================================================================================
FINDING 5: NO COLUMN GENERATION IN SHORT_AUCTIONS
============================================================================================

Solve_SMDAMAGE uses a column generation loop: it starts with a restricted set of bid steps
(from the solutions database) and adds columns with positive reduced cost until no more can
improve the objective. Short_auctions builds the full LP (all bid steps) from scratch on
every call.

With correct solver both should reach the same optimal. No impact on solution value.
Impact on solve time: short_auctions is much slower for large YEARS_IN_AUCTION because the
LP has ~250 bid years × bidders × bid steps columns all at once.

============================================================================================
RECOMMENDED FIXES
============================================================================================

(a) Fix the solver (Finding 1):
    Change line 634 from:
      PULP_CBC_CMD('HiGHS', msg=0)
    to:
      PULP_CBC_CMD(msg=0, options=['-threads', '8'])

(b) Fix the loop range (Finding 2):
    Change line 575 from:
      for startyear in range(2025, 2026):
    to match the original pattern (adjusted for current YEARS_IN_AUCTION), e.g.:
      for startyear in range(int(min(AllBidPeriods)), int(max(AllBidPeriods)) + 1, YEARS_IN_AUCTION):
    With YEARS_IN_AUCTION=200 this runs auctions at 2025 and 2225, covering all 250 bid years.

(c) Fix tau application at BeginConstraintYear (Finding 3):
    In the taxed temperature constraint, split the BidPeriods emitter sum at BeginConstraintYear,
    as Solve_SMDAMAGE does:
      local_tau * sum(W*v  for emitters with u in BidPeriods and u < BeginConstraintYear)
    + 1.0       * sum(W*v  for emitters with u in BidPeriods and u >= BeginConstraintYear)
    + 1.0       * sum(W*v  for removers in BidPeriods)

After fixing (a), the model should solve correctly. After fixing (b) and (c), the 200-year
single-auction result should be within a fraction of a percent of run_SMDAMAGE.
