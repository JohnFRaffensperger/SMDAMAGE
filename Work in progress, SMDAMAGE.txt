AUDIT (SECOND ROUND): Why removal cost for short auctions (Estimate 4) remains very high.
Written 2026-07-30.

================================================================================
STATUS OF PREVIOUS FIX
================================================================================

The previous fix (adding Fixed_Vpt contributions to the taxedTemperatureChange
constraint) is mathematically correct but makes no economic difference to
removal cost. Here is why.

At the end of each 2-year auction, the optimizer has achieved exact revenue
neutrality at the binding constraint year. Concretely, for auction covering
years [S, S+1], the LP drives:

    taxedTemperatureChange[2125] = tau*C_S*W(2125-S) + tau*C_{S+1}*W(2124-S)
                                    - A_S*W(2125-S) - A_{S+1}*W(2124-S) = 0

i.e., exactly balanced. So the contribution of Fixed_Vpt[S, S+1] to the next
auction's fixed term is exactly zero. The next auction again must independently
achieve tau*emissions = removals in its own 2-year window.

The fix is correct in structure: if any past auction happened to OVER-remove
(taxedTemp < 0), future auctions would get a credit and need less removal.
But the revenue-maximizing optimizer never voluntarily over-removes. It drives
taxedTemp to exactly 0. Credit is always zero. Total removal is unchanged.

================================================================================
THE REAL CAUSE: ECONOMIC DESIGN, NOT CODE BUG
================================================================================

The high removal cost is a direct consequence of how the short auction model
is designed. The comparison below makes this precise.

ESTIMATE 2 (run_SMDAMAGE, global model, ~commented out lines 790+)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
ONE LP over all 250 bid years simultaneously.
Revenue-neutrality constraint at t=2125 (and each t >= 2125):

    tau × SUM_{u=2025}^{2124} C_u × W(2125-u)   <=   SUM_{u=2025}^{2274} R_u × W(2125-u)

The optimizer sees all 250 years at once. It satisfies the CUMULATIVE
constraint by choosing the globally cheapest removals:
  - It allows max emissions in years where emitters bid high (valuable).
  - It accepts Agriculture from the years where Agriculture is CHEAPEST in
    discounted objective terms — which is the LATEST possible years (years
    2075-2124), where the discount factor is (1/1.03)^50 to (1/1.03)^99
    = 0.23 to 0.05 of the undiscounted price.
  - The Agriculture Vpt dual (shadow price) reflects the marginal cost of
    the globally cheapest accepted Agriculture, which is a LATE-YEAR,
    DEEPLY-DISCOUNTED removal step.

Result: small Vpt dual for Agriculture × moderate quantity = moderate cost.

ESTIMATE 4 (run_SMDAMAGE_short_auctions, 2-year windows)
---------------------------------------------------------
~125 sequential 2-year LPs.
Revenue-neutrality constraint in EACH WINDOW independently:

    tau × C_S × W(2125-S) + tau × C_{S+1} × W(2124-S)
        <=  A_S × W(2125-S) + A_{S+1} × W(2124-S) + (other removers in {S, S+1})

The optimizer in the 2025-2026 auction can ONLY use removers (Agriculture,
Seaweed, and forestry plantings) from years 2025 and 2026 to offset 2025-2026
emissions. It cannot use cheap removers from 2100, even if the warming effect
at t=2125 is the same.

WHY FORESTRY DOES NOT ESCAPE THIS CONSTRAINT:

Forestry (e.g., 100-year rotation Ponderosa pine, 103-year rotation) IS
included in the taxedTemp constraint for the 2025-2026 auction. The Wpt for
forestry planted in 2025 at lag 100 (= t=2125 - u=2025) is large: it reflects
100 years of accumulated carbon sequestration (e.g., ~5 tonnes/ha/yr for 100
years = ~500 tonnes/ha stored). Forestry IS available and the optimizer will
use it if it provides better value per Wpt unit than Agriculture.

However, forestry does not escape the fundamental problem for three reasons:

(a) DISCOUNTING: Forestry planted in 2025 must be paid for in 2025 at the
    full undiscounted bid price (discount factor = 1.0). The global model can
    instead wait and plant forestry in 2050 at a discounted price
    (factor ≈ (1/1.03)^25 = 0.48), using that cheaper future planting to
    satisfy a future constraint year. The short auction forces expensive NOW.

(b) LAND CONSTRAINT (correctly implemented, but still restrictive): Land
    committed in 2025-2026 is occupied for the full rotation period (R years)
    and is correctly released afterward — the condition `u <= t <= u + R - 1`
    in both fixed_land and the current planting sum ensures land is freed at
    t = u + R. However, during those R years, that land cannot be replanted.
    The global model allocates land optimally across all 250 years simultaneously,
    choosing the planting schedule that maximises cooling per unit of land over
    the full horizon. The short auction can only commit land in the current 2-year
    window, without knowing what future auctions will need.

(c) SHORT-ROTATION FORESTRY IS INEFFECTIVE AT LAG 100: The data includes
    10-year and 24-year rotation options whose sequestration drops to zero
    after harvest (e.g., Loblolly pine 10yr: tc=0 after year 10). At lag 100,
    only the fraction still in the atmosphere from those early years contributes.
    The binding constraint at t=2125 (lag 100) can only be satisfied well by
    long-rotation forestry (100+ years), which is exactly at the end of its
    rotation at t=2125. For t=2126 and beyond, the 100-year tree has been
    harvested; only the persisting carbon from years 1-100 continues to cool,
    at diminishing Wpt values. The optimizer must use Agriculture or Seaweed
    for the post-rotation constraint years (t >= 2126 for the 100-yr tree).

  - Years 2025-2026 Agriculture bids have discount factor = 1.0 (no discount).
    These are the MOST EXPENSIVE Agriculture bids in objective terms.
  - The auction must accept 1.7x emissions in removal-equivalent terms using
    only this expensive Agriculture.
  - The Agriculture Vpt dual = price of the marginal (threshold) Agriculture
    step accepted in 2025-2026, which is a HIGH-PRICED EARLY STEP.

Result: large Vpt dual for Agriculture in early years × large quantity = large cost.

================================================================================
QUANTITATIVE ILLUSTRATION
================================================================================

Suppose Agriculture's undiscounted bid price at the threshold step is $P/tC.

In Estimate 2:
  - The global optimizer accepts Agriculture primarily from year 2100 (approx).
  - Discounted bid price for year-2100 Agriculture = P × (1/1.03)^75 = 0.10 × P.
  - Vpt dual ≈ 0.10 × P.
  - Removal cost ≈ Q_total × 0.10P = small.

In short auctions (year 2025 auction):
  - Must accept 2025 Agriculture.
  - Discounted bid price for year-2025 Agriculture = P × (1/1.03)^0 = P.
  - Vpt dual ≈ P.
  - Year-2025 removal quantity ≈ tau × year-2025 emission = 1.7 × C_2025.
  - Contribution to removal cost ≈ 1.7 × C_2025 × P = large.

The Vpt dual in the year-2025 short auction is roughly 10× larger than in
Estimate 2. This alone explains most of the removal cost discrepancy.

================================================================================
WHY THE REMOVAL COST METRIC IS PARTICULARLY SENSITIVE
================================================================================

The Summary table computes:

    removal_cost = SUM over removers and years 2025-2125: quantity × Vpt_dual

This is total payment to removers at MARGINAL (dual) pricing, not at average
pricing. The Vpt dual in short auctions is set by the scarcity price:

    Vpt_dual(Agriculture, t) = |Agriculture bid price at threshold step| + lambda × Wpt

where lambda is the dual on the Capt(2125) constraint. In the 2-year auctions,
lambda is large (few removers available per window), inflating both the Vpt_dual
and the resulting removal cost.

================================================================================
THE RELEVANT COMPARISON
================================================================================

The short auctions model is economically LESS EFFICIENT than the global model
by construction: it cannot substitute cheap late-year removals for expensive
early-year emissions. This is a fundamental trade-off of the short-auction design.

The short auctions model represents a real-world constraint: in each 2-year
period, the market must be self-financing. Each auction cannot borrow against
future removal capacity. This forced use of expensive early removals is the
economic cost of the short-auction mechanism.

To make removal costs comparable to Estimate 2, one of the following would
be needed:
  (a) ALLOW TEMPORAL SUBSTITUTION: let early emissions generate "removal
      credits" that are assigned to future auction windows. The current auction
      balances against a long-run virtual price rather than physical removals
      in the current window.
  (b) USE A DYNAMIC TAU: each auction's tau is set to reflect the actual shadow
      price of removal in that window, not a fixed 1.7. When early removals are
      expensive, tau is set low (fewer credits per emission) to reduce removal cost.
  (c) USE A LONGER AUCTION WINDOW: instead of 2 years, use 10 or 25 years. This
      allows more temporal substitution within each window.

None of these are bugs — they are design choices. The current short auctions
design, which achieves per-window revenue neutrality using whatever removers are
available in that window, is more expensive by construction.

================================================================================
NOTE ON THE FIXED_VPT FIX (ALREADY APPLIED)
================================================================================

The fix correctly makes taxedTemperatureChange cumulative (accounting for past
auction contributions). This is the mathematically right structure. However, as
shown above, since each auction achieves exact balance (taxedTemp = 0 at the
binding constraint), the carried-forward contribution from past auctions is always
zero. The fix does not change the actual LP solutions or removal costs.

The fix would matter if:
  - Past auctions over-removed (taxedTemp < 0), providing credits.
  - But the revenue-maximizing optimizer never voluntarily over-removes.

================================================================================
TAU MISMATCH NOTE (MINOR, POST-2125 ONLY)
================================================================================

For Fixed_Vpt periods with u = 2125-2126 (the two years just past BeginConstraintYear
that still used local_tau=1.7 with the user's fix), the fixed term uses 1.0 × Wpt
rather than 1.7 × Wpt. This understates the past warming contribution by 0.7 × Wpt
for those 2 years. This means later auctions (2127+) think they have slightly more
removal headroom than they actually do. However:
  - This affects only years 2127+ (outside the 2025-2125 measurement window).
  - The impact on removal cost as reported is zero.
  - Fixing it would require tracking per-period tau history.
