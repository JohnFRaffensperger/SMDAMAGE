# SMDAMAGE Data Sources and Processing Documentation

This document describes the sources and processing steps for the emission and removal data used in SMDAMAGE.

## 1. Pollutant Data Sources

### Carbon (ffi_emissions)
- **Primary Source**: Anger, N., & Sathaye, J. (2008). "Reducing deforestation and trading emissions: Economic implications for the post-Kyoto period." SSRN-id1114044.
- **Details**: Specifically the Appendix for marginal abatement cost (MAC) curves across 25 regions and 2 sectors (Energy intensive and Non-energy intensive).
- **Global Scaling**: Total quantity scaled to match global 2022 fossil fuel emissions (~37,490 Mtons CO2 or ~10,232 Mtons C) based on Statista data and CDIAC conversion factors (1 ppm CO2 = 2.13 Gt C).
- **Reference URL**: [CDIAC Conversion Factors](https://cdiac.ess-dive.lbl.gov/pns/convert.html)

### Methane (CH4) & Nitrous Oxide (N2O)
- **Primary Source**: Harmsen, J.H.M., et al. (2015). "How many non-CO2 greenhouse gas mitigation measures would we need to achieve the 2°C target?" *Climate Policy*.
- **Data File**: `mmc1.xlsx` (Supplementary material).
- **Details**: Marginal abatement costs for non-CO2 gases. Baseline burdens guesstimated from Hector and NOAA AGGI.
- **Reference URL**: [NOAA AGGI](https://www.esrl.noaa.gov/gmd/aggi/aggi.html)

### Other Chemicals (C2F6, CF4, HFC125, HFC134a, HFC143a, SF6)
- **Primary Source**: Harmsen et al. (2015) supplementary data (`mmc2.xlsx`), https://www-sciencedirect-com.ezp.lib.rochester.edu/science/article/pii/S2352340919306882?ref=pdf_download&fr=RR-2&rr=a0f47e292e6a8c35
- **Details**: kt-scale emissions for fluorinated gases (HFCs, PFCs, SF6).
- **Managed Substances**: Some substances (e.g., CFC-11, CFC-12) are noted as "Controlled" under international protocols.

### Agriculture
- **Primary Source**: Smith, P. et al. (2014). "Agriculture, Forestry and Other Land Use (AFOLU)." In: *Climate Change 2014: Mitigation of Climate Change* (IPCC AR5 WGIII), Figure 11.17.
- **Details**: GtCO2/year mitigation potential for OECD, EIT, and other regions at $20, $50, and $100/ton CO2.
- **Reference**: IPCC AR5 WGIII Chapter 11.

### Seaweed
- **Primary Source**: Krause-Jensen, D., & Duarte, C. M. (2016). "Substantial role of macroalgae in marine carbon sequestration." *Nature Geoscience*.
- **Details**: Global potential of 48 million sq km (~4800 million ha).
- **Processing**: Max area limited to 48 million sq km; removal rate of ~3.03 tons C/ha/year.

### Forestry
- **Source for my 2021 paper**: Stavins, R. N., & Richards, K. R. (2005). "The cost of US forest-based carbon sequestration." Pew Center on Global Climate Change.
- **Source for my 2026 paper**: Busch (2024) SQLite database (`Busch2024_to_SMDAMAGE.sqlite`), which provides clustered bidders, bids, and carbon schedules.

## 2. Data Processing and Bid Curve Construction

The bid curves are constructed as piecewise linear functions (step functions) where each "bid" represents a tranche of mitigation or removal at a specific price.

### Global Carbon MAC Curve (ffi_emissions)
- **Calculation**: Data for 25 regions and 2 sectors were aggregated. To create a continuous global curve, the data was organized into **10Mt C tranches**.
- **Linearity**: The spreadsheet uses a linear combination of the regional MAC curves.
- **Bid Steps**: **412 steps** are defined in [MtC_bid_steps.csv](Data/MtC_bid_steps.csv).
- **Adjustments**:
  - Prices: Inflated from 2005 EUR to 2022 USD (Rate: 2.28%/year; Conversion: 1.1 USD/EUR).
  - Quantity: Scaled by a global factor (approx 3.74) to reach the 2022 emission baseline of 10,232 MtC.
  - Baseline: A 100 MtC tranche is added at $0 to represent cost-neutral "low-hanging fruit."

### Non-CO2 Gases (CH4, N2O, Chemicals)
- **Calculation**: Bid steps were derived from the Harmsen et al. (2015) datasets.
- **Linearity**: The sources provide discrete price/quantity pairs; the spreadsheets maintain these as discrete steps.
- **Bid Steps**:
  - **CH4**: **201 steps** in [CH4_bid_steps.csv](Data/CH4_bid_steps.csv).
  - **N2O**: **201 steps** in [N2O_bid_steps.csv](Data/N2O_bid_steps.csv).
  - **F-Gases (C2F6 to SF6)**: Combined into **201 steps** in [C2F6_CF4_HFC125_HFC134a_HFC143a_SF6_bidsteps.csv](Data/C2F6_CF4_HFC125_HFC134a_HFC143a_SF6_bidsteps.csv).

### Agriculture Removal
- **Calculation**: Based on IPCC AR5 Figure 11.17, which provides mitigation potential at $20, $50, and $100/ton CO2.
- **Linearity**: The spreadsheet applies **linear interpolation** (or a simple linear fit) between the IPCC price points to generate a more granular curve. Note that prices are negative (-$20, -$50, -$100).
- **Bid Steps**: **57 steps** in [Agriculture_bids.csv](Data/Agriculture_bids.csv).

### Seaweed Removal
- **Calculation**: Global area (4,800m ha) is divided into equal tranches. The spreadsheet calculates $Million/million ha costs.
- **Linearity**: The cost function is **perfectly linear**, increasing by approx -$60.35 per 10 million hectare tranche.
- **Bid Steps**: **24 steps** in [Seaweed_bids.csv](Data/Seaweed_bids.csv), capping at the global area limit.

### Forestry Sequestration
- See the separate Busch forestry project.

## 3. Plan for prepare_inputs.py

The objective is to replace the manual Excel processing with a reproducible Python script that writes directly to `smdamage_data.db`.

### 1. Requirements
- Use `pandas` for data manipulation and `openpyxl` for reading original Excel supplements.
- Use `sqlite3` for database interaction.
- Centralize constants (inflation rates, CO2->C conversion factor 3.66406, ha->acre 0.404687, etc.) in a `defaults.py` or similar.

### 2. Processing Modules
- **`process_carbon()`**: Automate the extraction from Anger & Sathaye (2008) Appendix, inflationary adjustment, and scaling to global FFI levels.
- **`process_harmsen()`**: Batch process `mmc1.xlsx` and `mmc2.xlsx` for CH4, N2O, and HFC/PFC/SF6 gases.
- **`process_agriculture()`**: Convert IPCC AR5 mitigation values (GtCO2) to negative-priced carbon removal bids (MtC).
- **`process_seaweed()`**: Generate bid curves for seaweed sequestration based on global area limits and productivity constants.

### 3. Database Execution
- The script will bypass CSV generation and directly update `smdamage_data.db`:
  - `INSERT` into `bidders` table (class, units, hector_name).
  - `INSERT` into `bids` table (price_per_unit, quantity_units).
  - Log summary statistics (total available mitigation/sequestration per pollutant) for validation.

### 4. Integration with create_database.py
- `prepare_inputs.py` will handle the "upstream" logic previously done in Excel.
- `create_database.py` will focus on the "downstream" logic (e.g., pulling from Hector for warming factors and pulling from Busch for forestry).
- Ideally, `prepare_inputs.py` would be called as the first step in a `rebuild_database.ps1` workflow.
